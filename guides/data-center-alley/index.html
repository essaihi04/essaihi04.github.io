Data Center Alley: why the AI cloud lives in Loudoun County, Virginia

⬇ Download the PDF

The short version: fiber came first, then the networks, then the rules that let buildings go up fast.

The map in 5 steps

  1. 1992 — Vienna, Virginia. Engineers link local fiber networks into MAE East, the first commercial internet exchange point (where networks meet and swap traffic). *(Loudoun County; Wikipedia: MAE-East)*
  2. 1996 — Ashburn. AOL opens its global headquarters in Ashburn, Loudoun County. UUNet follows in 1998. *(Loudoun County)*
  3. 1997 — half the world. About half of the world's internet traffic passes through MAE East. *(Loudoun Economic Development)*
  4. 1998 — into Loudoun. The exchange moves to Loudoun. Networks want to sit next to other networks: fiber attracts fiber. *(Loudoun Economic Development)*
  5. 2000 — built like offices. The county's Zoning Administrator treats data centers like office buildings, so they can be built "by right" wherever offices are allowed. (By-right development was ended in 2025.) *(Loudoun County)*

The numbers today (Loudoun County, as of March 2026)

Why here? The 4 reasons the county itself lists

  • Early fiber and the internet exchange right next door.
  • A reliable power grid.
  • Close to Washington, D.C., federal agencies and a major airport (Dulles).
  • A business-friendly permitting environment and a qualified workforce.

Words to know

  • Data center: a building full of servers. Your AI chats, videos and cloud files run on machines like these.
  • Internet exchange point (IXP): a place where many networks plug into each other to swap traffic directly.
  • By right: a project allowed by the zoning rules, with no special public vote needed.
