8 Free AI Sites: the list with links

⬇ Download the PDF

Facts below come from the sources listed at the end. Free tiers change, so check each site's current terms before relying on it.

1. Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) — https://notebooklm.google.com

  • Google Labs tool: it answers from the sources you give it (websites, PDFs, Google Docs, Slides, YouTube transcripts).
  • Audio Overviews (Sept 2024) turn your sources into a podcast-style chat between two AI hosts.
  • Guides published in 2026 list 50 sources per notebook on the free plan (secondary sources; verify on Google's help page).
  • Try it: upload one long PDF you already own and ask three questions.

2. Arena (formerly LMArena / Chatbot Arena) — https://arena.ai

  • Started April 2023 at UC Berkeley. You send one prompt, two anonymous models answer, you vote, names are revealed after.
  • Votes feed a public leaderboard. Core use is free. Votes can be biased toward confident-sounding answers.
  • Try it: send the same real work question and vote before looking at the names.

3. Google AI Studio — https://aistudio.google.com

  • Launched Dec 13, 2023 with the Gemini API. Free to use; the API has a free tier with daily and per-minute limits.
  • Catch: on the free tier Google may use your prompts and files to improve its products. Never paste private data.
  • Try it: test a prompt with a made-up example, not a real contract.

4. Teachable Machine — https://teachablemachine.withgoogle.com

  • Free, no code. Train an image, sound or pose model in your browser using your webcam or microphone.
  • Try it: train "pen" vs "mug", then hold each one up.

5. goblin.tools — https://goblin.tools

  • Small tools: Magic ToDo, Formalizer, Judge, Estimator, Compiler, Chef. The site says it is kept free by its sponsors.
  • Try it: paste one scary task into Magic ToDo.

6. Semantic Scholar — https://www.semanticscholar.org

  • Built by the Allen Institute for AI, public since Nov 2, 2015. Free. TLDR summaries in its reader.
  • A 2026 product page lists 214 million papers.
  • Try it: search the real paper behind a headline you saw this week.

7. Hugging Face Spaces — https://huggingface.co/spaces

  • Hugging Face was founded in New York in 2016. Anyone can try Spaces demos in a browser.
  • DALL·E Mini ran as a Space in 2022 and became Craiyon in June 2022.
  • Creating: static Spaces are free; Gradio/Docker Spaces need a paid plan, with a small free ZeroGPU allowance for free accounts.

8. Quick, Draw! — https://quickdraw.withgoogle.com

  • Google Creative Lab and Data Arts, released Nov 14, 2016. Six rounds, 20 seconds each, a neural network guesses.
  • About 50 million drawings form an open dataset (CC BY 4.0); the only personal data saved is a two-letter country code.
