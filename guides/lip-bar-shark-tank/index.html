The Lip Bar: the Shark Tank deal sheet

⬇ Download the PDF

All five Sharks passed on The Lip Bar in 2015. Here is what was on the table, and what happened next. Every figure below comes from a named source.

The pitch

  • Show: Shark Tank (ABC), season 6, episode 18, aired in 2015.
  • Founders on stage: Melissa Butler (founder and CEO) and Rosco Spears (creative director).
  • Product: vegan lipstick, sold online. Butler started the brand in 2012, making lipstick at home in Brooklyn.
  • The ask: $125,000 for 20% of the company.
  • Implied valuation: $125,000 ÷ 20% = $625,000.

What the Sharks said

  • Robert Herjavec was the first one out.
  • Kevin O'Leary: "You only have so many minutes on Earth, don't waste them trying to sell lipstick."
  • Daymond John: "You are never going to create anything new in this world. It's lipstick."
  • Result: no deal. All five Sharks passed.

After the Tank

  1. 2016: The Lip Bar appears on Target's website.
  2. February 18, 2018: launch in 44 Target stores. By July 2018: 142 stores (CNBC). Butler reached a Target buyer through LinkedIn.
  3. September 2022: the parent company raises an oversubscribed $6.7 million round.
  4. 2023: Target space grows from 3 shelves to 24 (+700%). CVS adds the brand in 3,300 stores.
  5. October 2025: about 2,000 stores across Target, CVS and Walmart, plus Amazon. Target sales are down about 30-40%, but the company expects total sales up about 40%, helped by its own website (+150%).

3 things this case shows

  • A "no" on TV is not a "no" from the market. The Lip Bar kept selling online after the show.
  • Go straight to the buyer. Butler found a retail buyer on LinkedIn instead of waiting for an investor.
  • Start small in retail, then grow the shelf. 44 stores in 2018, then more doors and more shelf space each year.

2 traps to watch

  • One big retailer can hurt you. When Target sales fell 30-40% in 2025, the brand needed other channels.
  • Don't confuse store count with sales. The company does not publish its revenue: "2,000 stores" is a distribution figure, not a valuation.

Glossary

  • Equity: a share of ownership. 20% of The Lip Bar = one fifth of the company.
  • Valuation: what the company is worth, implied by the ask. $125,000 for 20% means $625,000.
  • Doors: retail stores carrying a product. 44 doors in February 2018.
  • Direct-to-consumer (DTC): selling on your own website. The Lip Bar's site was up 150% in 2025.
