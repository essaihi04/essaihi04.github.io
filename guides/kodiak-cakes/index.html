Kodiak Cakes: the deal sheet

⬇ Download the PDF

Source note: figures come from Wikipedia, Food Republic, Looper, Trilantic, BizWest and Nosh. Investor or company claims are labeled as such.

The deal at a glance

  • Shark Tank US, Season 5, Episode 22 (April 2014)
  • The ask: $500,000 for 10% of the company, a $5 million valuation ($500,000 ÷ 10%)
  • Robert Herjavec: $500,000 for 35% (about $1.43M valuation)
  • Kevin O'Leary and Barbara Corcoran together: $500,000 for 50% (a $1M valuation)
  • Result: no deal. The founders declined both offers.

What happened next

  • Estimated $1 million in sales in the six weeks after airing (founder estimate)
  • Annual sales: $3.6M in 2013, $6.7M in 2014
  • June 2014: Protein Power Cakes launched, 14 g of protein per serving
  • 2016: Sunrise Strategic Partners, minority investment (amount undisclosed)
  • 2018: best-selling pancake mix brand at Target
  • May 2021: L Catterton buys a majority stake (price undisclosed; an $800M rumor is unconfirmed)
  • Reported annual revenue: over $200 million around 2021 (company/investor-reported, not audited)

3 things worth noticing

  1. Demand was proven before the TV moment: Kodiak was already on Target shelves.
  2. The founders knew what their company was worth to them and turned down 35% and 50% offers.
  3. A product launch (Protein Power Cakes) kept new customers after the TV spike.

2 mistakes to avoid

  1. Treating a "no deal" as a verdict on the business.
  2. Treating this story as a rule: many founders who refuse offers still fail.

Glossary

  • Valuation: what the whole company is worth = amount ÷ share. $500,000 for 10% means $5M.
  • Equity: the share of a company you own.
  • Slotting fee: money a brand pays a store for shelf space.
  • Minority stake: an investor owns less than half; founders keep control.
