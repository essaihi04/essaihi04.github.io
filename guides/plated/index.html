Plated: the Shark Tank deal sheet

⬇ Download the PDF

Key facts

  • Founders: Nick Taranto and Josh Hix, Harvard Business School classmates; company founded in 2012.
  • Shark Tank US, season 5, episode 22 (aired April 4, 2014).
  • The ask: $500,000 for 4% (a $12.5M valuation).
  • Mark Cuban offered $500,000 for about 5.56% plus advisory shares (about a $9M valuation). It was accepted on air but never closed.
  • Kevin O'Leary invested later (amount undisclosed).
  • Series B: $35 million, July 2015.
  • Acquisition: Albertsons, September 2017. The price is reported as $200 million (TechCrunch) to $300 million (Wikipedia, Mashed): outlets disagree.
  • November 2019: Albertsons ended the home-delivery subscription; last delivery November 27, 2019. The brand itself was kept for in-store kits.

3 things this case shows

  1. A deal agreed on air is not a signed deal: terms can change after filming.
  2. Raising from several investors can replace one missing deal.
  3. Being acquired is not the same as the product surviving.

2 mistakes to avoid

  1. Treating a TV handshake as money in the bank.
  2. Confusing the acquisition price with what the company is worth later.

Glossary

  • Valuation: amount invested divided by the share received ($500,000 / 4% = $12.5M).
  • Series B: a later venture round, after the first outside rounds.
  • Advisory shares: a small equity grant paid for an investor's advice.

Sources: Wikipedia "Plated (meal kits)"; TechCrunch (Sept 20, 2017); Mashed (Oct 7, 2022); FoodNavigator (Nov 13, 2019). Educational, not investment advice.
