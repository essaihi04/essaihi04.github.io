The Grinds Deal Sheet: a Shark Tank deal that died, and the company that didn't

⬇ Download the PDF

*Fallen Empires · After the Tank. Facts below come from the sources listed at the end. Observations about building a company, not investment advice.*

The deal at a glance

  • Company: Grinds, chewable coffee pouches. Founders: Matt Canepa and Pat Pezet.
  • Shark Tank US, Season 4.
  • The ask: $75,000 for 10% of the company. That values Grinds at $750,000 ($75,000 ÷ 10%).
  • Kevin O'Leary: offered a loan instead of equity.
  • Robert Herjavec and Daymond John: offered the full $75,000 for 15%, a valuation of $500,000 ($75,000 ÷ 15%). The founders accepted on camera.
  • After the show: the deal fell apart in negotiation (Forbes, 2016). The reasons were not published.
  • Sales: $1.35 million in the year the episode aired; about $4 million expected in 2016 (Forbes, 2016).

3 things worth noticing

  1. Demand came before the Sharks. The company was already selling before it pitched; the deal did not create the business.
  2. A TV deal is not a closed deal. Due diligence and negotiation happen after the cameras stop. Forbes found that a large share of Shark Tank deals do not close as shown.
  3. Exposure moved sales. Sales jumped in the year the episode aired, with or without the investment.

2 mistakes to avoid

  1. Counting the TV handshake as money in the bank. Plan the next steps as if the deal might not close.
  2. Giving up equity you don't need to give. Accepting 15% instead of 10% meant a lower valuation ($500,000 vs $750,000).

Glossary

  • Valuation: what the company is worth, implied by the deal: amount ÷ share. Here, $75,000 ÷ 10% = $750,000.
  • Equity: the share of the company an investor gets in return for money.
  • Due diligence: the investor's check of the books, contracts and claims before the money moves.
