What a hallucination is

An AI answer that sounds confident and fluent but is false or invented. Language models predict the most likely next words; they do not look facts up, so a made-up case or quote can read just as smoothly as a real one (OpenAI, "Why language models hallucinate", 2025).

Why it happens

  1. Prediction, not lookup: the model generates plausible text word by word.
  2. Tests reward guessing: OpenAI researchers argue most evaluations give credit for a lucky guess and nothing for answering "I don't know", so models learn to guess.

The real example

In *Mata v. Avianca* (S.D.N.Y., 2023), lawyers filed a brief citing court cases that ChatGPT had invented. Judge P. Kevin Castel fined the lawyers and their firm $5,000 (22 June 2023).

The 5-step check

  1. Ask for sources for every name, number, date and quote.
  2. Open each source yourself. If the link or document does not exist, the claim is unverified.
  3. Search the exact quote or title in a normal search engine.
  4. Cross-check important facts with a second, independent source.
  5. Give the AI permission to say "I don't know", for example: "If you are not sure, say so instead of guessing."

Red flags

  • Very specific details (case numbers, page numbers, statistics) with no link.
  • Sources that "almost" match a real title.
  • A confident tone on an obscure topic.
