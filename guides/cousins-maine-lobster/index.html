Cousins Maine Lobster: the Shark Tank deal, broken down

⬇ Download the PDF

Dramatized recap. All figures come from the sources listed at the end; "reported" means a press report, not an audited number.

The deal card

  • Show: Shark Tank US, Season 4, Episode 6 (aired October 2012)
  • Founders: Jim Tselikis and Sabin Lomac (cousins, Maine)
  • Ask: $55,000 for 5% = implied valuation of $1.1 million
  • Deal: Barbara Corcoran, $55,000 for 15% (she first asked for 17%, reported) = implied valuation of about $367,000
  • Other offer: Robert Herjavec also made an offer (terms not confirmed)
  • After: reported sales of about $20 million and franchising across the country

How to read the valuation

Valuation = amount ÷ share. $55,000 ÷ 0.05 = $1,100,000. $55,000 ÷ 0.15 = about $366,667.

The cousins gave up three times the equity they planned, so they accepted a much lower valuation in exchange for a partner they wanted.

3 things this case shows

  1. They told producers they would only appear if Barbara Corcoran was in the room: they chose a partner, not just money.
  2. A simple, repeatable product (a lobster roll from a truck) was easy to copy to new cities.
  3. Franchising let the brand grow without the founders funding every truck.

2 mistakes to avoid

  1. Treating the valuation you ask for as the valuation you will get: negotiation can cut it sharply.
  2. Mixing up sales and valuation: $20 million in reported sales is not a $20 million company value.

Glossary

  • Equity: the share of the company an investor owns.
  • Valuation: what the whole company is worth, implied by price ÷ share.
  • Franchise: other owners pay to run locations under your brand.
