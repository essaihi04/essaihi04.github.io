Dude Wipes on Shark Tank: the full deal sheet

⬇ Download the PDF

From the Fallen Empires short "Mark Cuban Bet on Flushable Wipes for Guys". Every number below is sourced and dated. This is a business case study, not investment advice.

The pitch (Season 7, aired October 16, 2015)

  • Company: Dude Products (DUDE Wipes), founded by friends in a Chicago apartment (Sean Riley, Jeff Klimkowski, Ryan Meegan, Brian Wilkin).
  • Product: flushable wipes marketed to men.
  • The ask: $300,000 for 10% of the company, which values it at $3 million ($300,000 ÷ 10%).
  • What they already had: Dude Wipes were already sold at Kroger nationwide before the episode aired.

The deal

  • Mark Cuban first said he was out, then came back with his own offer.
  • Final deal: Mark Cuban, $300,000 for 25%, which values the company at $1.2 million ($300,000 ÷ 25%).
  • The founders gave up 2.5 times more equity than they asked to give, at a valuation 60% lower.

3 things this case shows

  1. Retail traction before the pitch. A national Kroger listing made the product credible before any shark said yes.
  2. One hero product. In 2020 the brand tested a deodorant; co-founder Sean Riley later called it "a strategic mistake" and the team refocused on wipes (CNBC, 2021).
  3. A brand voice that matches the buyer. A product made for men, in a category where they rarely shop, with humor on the pack.

2 mistakes to avoid

  1. Spreading too thin. Cuban's advice in 2015 was "Don't drown in opportunity" (CNBC, 2021).
  2. Fixating on valuation alone. The founders accepted a lower valuation for the right partner; CFO Jeff Klimkowski said Cuban's offer was "the deal we wanted" (CFO.com, 2024).

Glossary

  • Equity: the share of the company an investor owns. Here: 25%.
  • Valuation: what the whole company is worth in the deal. $300,000 for 25% = $1.2 million.
  • Gross sales: total sales before returns and discounts.
  • Revenue: money the company brings in from sales; not the same as profit.
  • Retail distribution: getting a product onto store shelves (Kroger, Target, Walmart).
