The Lip Bar: the full deal sheet

⬇ Download the PDF

From a Brooklyn kitchen to Shark Tank rejection to thousands of store shelves. Every number below comes from a named source (listed at the end). This is a business case study, not investment advice.

The pitch (Shark Tank, Season 6, Episode 18, 2015)

  • Founders: Melissa Butler (founder and CEO, former Barclays analyst) and Rosco Spears (creative director).
  • Product: vegan lipsticks in bright colors and nudes, first made by hand in Brooklyn in 2012.
  • Sales before the show: about $27,000 in year one; just over $100,000 in roughly three years.
  • The ask: $125,000 for 20%.
  • Implied valuation: $125,000 ÷ 20% = $625,000.
  • Result: no deal. All five Sharks declined (Herjavec, Greiner, John, Cuban, O'Leary).

Timeline after the Tank

  1. 2015: Butler rewatches the episode and decides to pitch Target. A former Target beauty buyer, found on LinkedIn, passes the pitch on. Silence. About four months later, a second message to a current Target employee gets an answer.
  2. 2016: The Lip Bar launches on Target.com.
  3. 18 Feb 2018: 44 Target stores. July 2018: 142 stores.
  4. July 2018: first outside investment, New Voices Fund (backed by Unilever), $2 million.
  5. 2019: flagship store in downtown Detroit.
  6. 2021: Walmart.
  7. 2022: Detroit billboards: "Shark Tank told me to quit. 10 years and 2 million units sold. Thanks, Mr. Wonderful." $6.7 million round led by Pendulum, with The Fearless Fund and Endeavor.
  8. 2022-2023: the brand nearly runs out of capital (Butler, Beauty Independent).
  9. Feb 2023: Target shelf space from 3 to 24 shelves (+700%).
  10. Sept 2023: 3,300 CVS stores; about 5,300 physical doors in total.
  11. Oct 2025: about 2,000 stores (Target, Walmart, CVS) plus Amazon and TikTok Shop. Target sales down 30-40%, total sales expected up 40%, website sales up 150%, EBITDA expected to double.

3 things that worked

  • Real demand before scale: $27,000 from real customers in year one, before any investor.
  • Using criticism as data: the Sharks' best point (you can't see a lipstick's true color online) was solved by a store shelf.
  • Going around the gatekeeper: a LinkedIn search and a second message opened Target.

2 mistakes to avoid

  • Growing faster than cash: every new shelf needs stock paid months before the store pays you; the brand nearly ran dry in 2022-2023.
  • Depending on one retailer: when Target sales fell 30-40%, growth had to come from the website, Amazon and Walmart.

Glossary

  • Valuation: what the whole company is said to be worth. Example: $125,000 for 20% means $625,000.
  • Equity: a share of ownership. The Sharks were offered 20% of The Lip Bar.
  • Retail distribution: getting a product onto store shelves. Example: 44 Target stores in Feb 2018, 142 by July.
  • Direct-to-consumer (DTC): selling through your own website. The Lip Bar's site sales grew 150% in 2025.
  • EBITDA: earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization; a rough measure of operating profit.
