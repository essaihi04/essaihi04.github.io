Toymail on Shark Tank: the deal sheet

⬇ Download the PDF

A talking plush toy, two sharks, a $12 million valuation. Here is what is on the record.

The deal

  • Episode: Shark Tank season 8, episode 17 (aired February 17, 2017).
  • Founder in the Tank: Gauri Nanda (co-founder: Audry Hill).
  • Product: "Talkies", plush toys that let kids send and receive voice messages with family through a parent app. No phone or screen for the child.
  • The ask: $250,000 for 2.5% of the company, a $10 million valuation ($250,000 ÷ 2.5%).
  • The offers: Chris Sacca opened at $400,000 for 5%, then raised to $600,000 for 5%, split with Lori Greiner.
  • On air: Nanda accepted. Deal valuation: $600,000 ÷ 5% = $12 million.

Before the Tank

  • Kickstarter (fall 2013): just over $80,000 raised.
  • About $1.5 million seed from Amazon's Alexa Fund, Verizon and angel investors.
  • Y Combinator, Winter 2016 batch.
  • More than 20,000 toys sold by early 2016.

Today

  • Y Combinator lists Toymail as inactive.
  • We found no reliable public record of when or why it stopped. Fan sites mention a 2018 bankruptcy and a deal that never closed; we could not confirm either in a reliable source, so we do not repeat them as fact.

3 things this case shows

  1. Prove demand before TV. Toymail had crowdfunding, sales and strategic investors before its pitch.
  2. Strategic backers open doors. Amazon's voice fund and a phone carrier fit a toy built on voice messages.
  3. A higher valuation is possible in the Tank. Competing interest moved the offer from $400,000 to $600,000.

2 mistakes to avoid

  1. Confusing hype with a durable business. Big names on the cap table do not guarantee that the company lasts.
  2. Ignoring unit costs. On air, each toy cost about $20 to make and retailed around $50 to $59: hardware margins leave little room for error.

Glossary

  • Valuation: what the whole company is worth in a deal. Example: $600,000 for 5% = $12 million.
  • Equity: the share of the company an investor owns. Here, 5%.
  • Seed round: the first outside investment after founders and friends. Toymail's was about $1.5 million.
  • Cost of goods (COGS): what it costs to make one unit. Toymail: about $20 per toy.
