Lovepop on Shark Tank: the deal sheet

⬇ Download the PDF

Two naval architects turned ship-design techniques into 3D pop-up greeting cards, and took them to Shark Tank.

Every number below comes from the sources listed at the end. This is a business case study, not investment advice.

The deal

| | |

|---|---|

| Episode | Shark Tank, Season 7, holiday episode, first aired December 11, 2015 |

| Founders | Wombi Rose (CEO) and John Wise, naval architects who met at the Webb Institute, then reunited at Harvard Business School |

| Product | 3D pop-up cards cut from paper, using "Slicegami": kirigami combined with the slice-form structure used in ship design |

| The ask | $300,000 for 10% (asked valuation: $3 million) |

| Sharks out | Mark Cuban, Lori Greiner, Barbara Corcoran |

| Bidders | Robert Herjavec bid; Kevin O'Leary matched him |

| Final deal | Kevin O'Leary: $300,000 for 15% (deal valuation: $2 million) |

| Closed? | Yes. Lovepop joined O'Leary's "Something Wonderful" group (press release, Dec 14, 2015) |

What happened next

  • 2018: $12.5 million Series A (Accomplice, Highland Capital Partners).
  • 2019: a $10.1 million follow-on round. About $22.6 million raised in two rounds.
  • 2020–2022: six company-owned stores opened in two years, two of them at Disney locations (Downtown Disney, Disney Springs).
  • May 2022: Lovepop cards in more than 400 Walmart stores plus walmart.com, its first mass-retail partnership.
  • Other major partners named in 2022: Kohl's and Barnes & Noble, among hundreds of retailers.

3 things Lovepop did right

  1. Turned a real skill into a product edge. The founders used the ship-design structure they trained on, so the cards are hard to copy by hand.
  2. Proved demand before TV. They were already selling through kiosks in Boston before the episode aired.
  3. Picked the partner, not just the price. They accepted 15% instead of 10% to work with the Shark they chose.

2 mistakes to avoid (lessons from the case)

  1. Judging a deal only by the equity given up. Lovepop gave 5 more points than it asked for; what came after mattered more than that gap.
  2. Staying in one channel. Lovepop sold online, in its own stores and through big retailers; relying on a single channel is riskier.

Glossary

  • Valuation: what the company is worth in the deal. $300,000 ÷ 10% = $3 million asked; $300,000 ÷ 15% = $2 million agreed.
  • Equity: the share of the company the investor gets (here, 15%).
  • Series A: the first big round of venture capital after the early days ($12.5 million for Lovepop in 2018).
  • Retail / mass retail: selling through other stores (Walmart, Kohl's) instead of only your own website.
