1. Gemini 4 Hydrogen (Google)

  • What it is: the fast Gemini 4 model. Carbon is the deep thinker; Hydrogen is the everyday worker.
  • Speed: finishes tasks 2.5× faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash (Google).
  • Price: $1.50 per million input tokens, $7.50 per million output tokens, flat across the 1-million-token context window.
  • Terminal-Bench 4.1: 57% (Gemini 3.8 Flash: 18.2%).
  • Where: Gemini app (auto model selection), Google Search, AI Studio and the Gemini API.
  • Try it for: turning PDFs, spreadsheets, charts, audio and video into a report with citations.

2. Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 (Anthropic)

  • What it is: one model, two versions. Fable is for everyone; Mythos is for vetted security and biomedical teams.
  • Price: $8 per million input tokens, $40 per million output tokens. Outputs up to 30% faster than 5.1.
  • FrontierSWE v2: 69.9% (Claude Opus 5.5: 62.3%).
  • Tested on: a Python → Rust migration (≈26× faster start-up), a prototype OS built by 1,000+ subagents in 8 days, a Lean formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours.
  • Where: Claude apps, Claude Code, the Claude Platform (claude-fable-5-2), AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure.
  • Try it for: the long, hard job. Let it act as advisor or orchestrator and hand simple steps to smaller models.

3. GitHub Copilot Fusion (+ GPT-6.2)

  • What it is: a router. You pick an effort level; Copilot picks and coordinates the models for each step.
  • Terminal-Bench 4.1: Fusion Max 71.2%, 5.4 points ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 (GitHub).
  • Cost: 88% less than Fable 5.2 and 57% less than Opus 5.5 (GitHub).
  • New in the mix: Claude Sonnet 5.5 (Fusion Max), GPT-6.1 Terra (Fusion High), MAI-Code-2 (Fusion Standard).
  • GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol: generally available in Copilot (gradual rollout) for Pro+, Max, Business and Enterprise. In GitHub's early tests, GPT-6.2 Sol found nearly 2× as many real code-review issues as GPT-6.1 Sol.
  • Try it for: everyday coding. Track your cost per finished task, not per token.

4. NVIDIA: the numbers and the hardware

  • Q3 fiscal 2027 revenue: $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year. Data center: $107.6 billion. Outlook for next quarter: about $130 billion (±3%).
  • Vera Rubin NVL72 (provisional MLPerf Endpoints result, Kimi K3): more than 10× the tokens per watt of GB300 NVL72 (41,414 vs 2,210 tokens/s).
  • DGX Spark HPC Developer Kit: 4 desktop nodes in a ring, no network switch, 512 GB combined memory, models up to 700B parameters run locally (NVFP4). On sale through OEM partners; no price published.
  • Takeaway: if your data can't leave the building, a frontier-size open model now fits in an office.

5. AlphaGenome 2 (Google DeepMind)

  • What it does: reads 1 million DNA letters at once and predicts how a change affects one specific cell type.
  • Trained on: 107+ million single-cell profiles across 68 tissues (human and mouse).
  • Result: found the causal variant in 30% of cases vs 16% for previous tools (TenK10K, 28 immune cell types).
  • Access: free for non-commercial research via the AlphaGenome API; commercial access in private preview on Google Cloud.

The one idea to remember

The hard part of AI work is no longer only raw intelligence. It is choosing which model does which step:

  1. A fast, cheap model for the easy steps (Hydrogen-class).
  2. An expensive expert for the hard thinking (Fable-class).
  3. A router or orchestrator that splits the work (Fusion-class).
