Breathometer: the deal sheet

⬇ Download the PDF

Source-checked facts about one of Shark Tank's most unusual deals.

The pitch

  • Show: Shark Tank US, Season 5 (listed as episode 2 by Shark Tank Blog).
  • Product: Breathometer, a smartphone-connected breathalyzer. Founder: Charles Michael Yim.
  • Ask: $250,000 for 10% (a $2.5M valuation).

The offers

  • Mark Cuban: $500,000 for 20%.
  • The Sharks then teamed up: $1,000,000 for 30% (a $3.3M valuation).
  • Did all five fund it? Reports conflict. Cuban said in 2022 that he was the only one who funded it.

What the FTC said (January 2017)

  • Devices: Original ($49) and Breeze ($99), sold 2013 to 2015.
  • The FTC said readings were often inaccurate and the company lacked scientific evidence for its accuracy claims.
  • The FTC put sales at $5.1 million; the settlement offered full refunds to buyers who asked.
  • The company and its CEO were barred from making accuracy claims for a consumer breathalyzer without rigorous testing.

3 things this case shows

  1. Claims about a product's accuracy need independent testing before they go in an ad.
  2. A TV deal announced on air can differ from the money that actually arrives after due diligence.
  3. Regulators look at what a company says in its marketing, not only at what it sells.

2 mistakes it illustrates

  1. Marketing a device as "law enforcement grade" without evidence.
  2. Keeping a product on the market after problems are known.

Glossary

  • Valuation: the price of the whole company implied by a deal (money ÷ share bought).
  • Equity: the percentage of the company an investor gets.
  • Settlement: an agreement that ends a legal action without a trial.
