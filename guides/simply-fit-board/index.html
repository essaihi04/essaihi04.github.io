Simply Fit Board: the Shark Tank deal sheet

⬇ Download the PDF

A mother-daughter balance board that took the smaller check on Shark Tank, then sold $174 million.

Educational case study, not investment advice.

The deal (Season 7, Episode 7, 2015)

  • Founders: Gloria Hoffman and Linda Clark (mother and daughter)
  • Product: the Simply Fit Board, a curved plastic balance board: you stand on it and twist (core and cardio)
  • Sales before the show: about $575,000
  • Ask: $125,000 for 15% (implied valuation: about $833,000)
  • Kevin O'Leary: $250,000 for 30% (same valuation, twice the cash)
  • Lori Greiner: $125,000 for 20%, alone (implied valuation: $625,000)
  • Result: the founders accepted Lori Greiner's offer. Some sources report the final stake as 18% instead of 20%.

After the show

  • The board went on QVC with Lori Greiner and sold out.
  • About seven months later: $9 million in sales (figure given by Lori Greiner).
  • 2021: sold in about 50,000 US retail stores (Lori Greiner's website).
  • October 2023: $174 million in lifetime sales, 14th among all Shark Tank companies (USA Today, Sony Pictures Television data; lorigreiner.com).

3 things that worked

  1. Proof before the pitch: $575,000 of sales showed real demand.
  2. Picking the right partner, not the biggest check: Lori Greiner brought QVC and retail distribution, the channel this product needed.
  3. A product you understand in one second: stand, twist, done. Easy to demo on live TV shopping.

2 mistakes to avoid

  1. Judging an offer only by its cash: O'Leary's offer had more money but cost twice the equity.
  2. Ignoring what the partner brings: money alone does not put a product on 50,000 store shelves.

Glossary

  • Equity: the share of the company the investor gets. 20% of Simply Fit = one fifth of every future profit.
  • Valuation: what the company is worth, implied by the deal. $125,000 / 15% = $833,333.
  • Retail distribution: getting a product onto store shelves. Simply Fit reached about 50,000 stores by 2021.
