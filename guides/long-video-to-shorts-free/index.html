Turn One Long Video Into Shorts With Free AI

👉 Open the link⬇ Download the PDF

The exact method from the video, tested on a 28-minute NASA film (1969, public domain). Total cost: $0. No GPU needed.

What you need

  • Python 3.9 or newer
  • FFmpeg (free): https://ffmpeg.org
  • faster-whisper (free, MIT licence), a faster version of OpenAI's Whisper: https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper
  • A video you have the rights to use (your own video, or public domain)

Step 1: install

pip install faster-whisper

Step 2: transcribe, with a time for every word

Save this as transcribe.py:

import json, sys
from faster_whisper import WhisperModel
model = WhisperModel("small.en", device="cpu", compute_type="int8")
segments, info = model.transcribe(sys.argv[1], word_timestamps=True, vad_filter=True)
out = [{"start": s.start, "end": s.end, "text": s.text.strip(),
        "words": [{"w": w.word, "s": w.start, "e": w.end} for w in s.words]} for s in segments]
json.dump(out, open("transcript.json", "w"), indent=1)
for s in out: print(f"{s['start']:7.1f}  {s['text']}")

Run it:

python transcribe.py my-video.mp4

In our test, 28 minutes of audio took 150 seconds on 4 CPU cores.

Tip: if faster-whisper cannot open your file, convert the audio first with ffmpeg -i my-video.mp4 -ac 1 -ar 16000 audio.wav and transcribe audio.wav.

Step 3: find your moments

Search the printed transcript (Ctrl+F) instead of scrubbing the video:

  • numbers ("383 pounds")
  • surprising words ("never", "first", "only")
  • questions and answers

Write down the start and end second of each moment (30 to 60 seconds is a good Short).

Step 4: cut a vertical clip (9:16) with one command

ffmpeg -ss 777.5 -to 838 -i my-video.mp4 -filter_complex "[0:v]split[a][b];[a]scale=1080:1920:force_original_aspect_ratio=increase,crop=1080:1920,boxblur=30:2[bg];[b]scale=1080:-2[fg];[bg][fg]overlay=0:(H-h)/2" -c:v libx264 -crf 20 -c:a aac clip1.mp4

This puts the original picture in the middle and a blurred copy behind it, so nothing is cropped away.

Step 5: burn in captions

Turn the word times from transcript.json into a subtitle file (.srt or .ass), then add ,subtitles=captions.srt at the end of the filter in Step 4. Three words per caption reads well on a phone.

3 ways to use it

  1. Podcast or interview: search for numbers and strong opinions, cut 5 clips per episode.
  2. Tutorial: search for "step" or "the trick is", one clip per step.
  3. Webinar or lecture: search for questions from the audience, one clip per answer.

2 mistakes to avoid

  • Posting without reading the captions: Whisper wrote "100-prison laser" instead of "100-prism laser" in our test.
  • Cutting mid-sentence: start at the segment's start time, end at the next segment's start.
